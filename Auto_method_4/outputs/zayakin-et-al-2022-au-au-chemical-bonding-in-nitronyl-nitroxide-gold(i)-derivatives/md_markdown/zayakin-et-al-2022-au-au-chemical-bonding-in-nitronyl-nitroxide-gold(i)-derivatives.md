ABSTRACT: Gold(I) derivatives of nitronyl nitroxide (NN) stabilized by complexation with phosphine ligands were designed to promote dimerization of the paramagnets in the solid state via Au−Au bonding. The complexes were successfully synthesized by the reaction of the corresponding phosphine $\left[n{\mathrm{-}}\mathrm{Bu}_{3}\mathrm{P}\right.$ or $(4\mathrm{-FC}_{6}\mathrm{\dot{H}}_{4})_{3}\mathrm{P}]$ , $\operatorname{AuCl}(\mathrm{THT})$ , and $\mathrm{NN{-}H}$ under basic conditions in high yields. The gold(I) complexes were characterized by cyclic voltammetry, electron spin resonance, infrared, and UV/vis spectroscopy supported by quantum chemical calculations. Crystallographic analyses of the complexes showed that they possess Au−Au bonds (2.930 and 3.095 Å). The Au−Au bonding was confirmed by density functional theory calculations and quantum theory of “atoms in molecules” and natural bond orbital analyses, which made it possible to determine bond critical points $(3,\,-1)$ and Au−Au bond orders (0.36 and 0.23) as well as to estimate the orbital contribution to the energy of Au−Au bonds (12.0 and $8.6\ \mathrm{kcal/mol}_{\mathrm{}}$ ).

# INTRODUCTION  

Metal−metal bonding $(\mathsf{M}\mathrm{-}\mathsf{M})$ imparts important properties to functional materials, for example, luminescence, polychromism, electrical conductivity, or magnetism.1−3 The reasons for the $\mathrm{M-M}$ bonding are related to the relativistic effects inherent in heavy elements. Most reports of metal−metal bonding deal with the solid state, where X-ray diffraction (XRD) structural parameters provide accurate information about metal−metal distances to reveal short ones. Such short distances are often observed in gold(I) complexes, generally referred to as $\mathrm{{Au}(I){-}A u(I)}$ bonding.4−6 This type of bond occurs in all areas of gold chemistry, especially in catalysis7 and photoluminescence.8,9 Complexes containing P-donor ligands occupy a prominent place in gold(I) coordination chemistry, and many structurally characterized examples feature the expected $\mathrm{{Au}(I){-}A u(I)}$ bonds.10,11 It has been found that even coordination compounds with an extended ligand framework assemble in the solid state in such a way that the $\mathrm{Au(I)-Au(I)}$ bonds can contribute to overall stability.  

Recently, a new trend has emerged in the chemistry of gold complexes with phosphine ligands. This trend is related to the synthesis and study of the properties of complexes in which a gold atom is bonded to a paramagnetic moiety. The first examples of such complexes are specific derivatives of a paramagnetic organogold unit with a phosphine ligand $\left(\mathrm{PR}_{3}\right)$ of the $\mathrm{NN-Au-PR}_{3}$ type (where NN is a deprotonated nitronyl nitroxide moiety, i.e., 4,4,5,5-tetramethylimidazoline3-oxide-1-oxyl).12−14 It has been revealed that they have unique redox properties and can serve as an attractive crosscoupling reagent for introducing the NN group into a reaction with aryl iodides and activated aryl bromides.12,15−25  

Nitronyl nitroxide−gold derivatives $\scriptstyle\left(\mathrm{NN-Au-PR}_{3}\right)$ are airstable and possess high kinetic stability both in solution and in the solid state. An analysis of their crystal structures has revealed that in $\mathrm{NN-Au-PR}_{3},$ , the nitronyl nitroxide moiety is prone to the formation of a large number of short intermolecular contacts as compared to purely organic nitronyl nitroxides.14 This tendency is due to the anionic nature of the NN group, which effectively interacts with positively charged atoms of neighboring molecules, thereby giving rise to a threedimensional structure. Moreover, the stability of $\mathrm{NN-Au-PR}_{3}$ paves a unique way to investigation into $\mathrm{Au(I)-Au(I)}$ bonding and related phenomena in open-shell molecules. To study Au− Au bonding, Japanese chemists have developed various dinuclear gold complexes with $o_{\mathrm{~,~}}m_{\mathrm{~-~}}$ , and $p$ -phenylene linkers (Scheme 1). Among the prepared complexes, the orthosubstituted derivatives in which $\mathbf{\bar{A}u}(\mathrm{I}){-}\mathbf{Au}(\bar{\mathrm{I}})$ bonds are clearly observed in an analysis of the crystal structure show characteristic spin−spin interactions, electrochemical properties, and solvatochromic behavior.26  

It can be assumed that the geometry of diphosphine ligands in ortho-substituted derivatives (Scheme 1) is the driving force behind $\mathrm{Au(I)-Au(I)}$ bonding. Nonetheless, a question arises, can these $\mathrm{{Au}(I){-}A u(I)}$ bonds be realized in principle without support of the ligand backbone? In this paper, we give an affirmative answer and describe the first nitronyl nitroxide− gold(I)-phosphine complexes $\begin{array}{r}{\left(\mathrm{NN-Au-PR}_{3}\right)}\end{array}$ ) capable of forming $\mathrm{\dot{Au}(\hat{I}){-}A u(I)}$ bonds in the solid state.

def2-TZVP basis set45 for all other atoms were employed in these calculations.  

In addition, the natural bond orbital $(\mathrm{NBO})^{46,47}$ analysis was performed in the NBO7 software48,49 and GAUSSIAN16 software suite.50 In the NBO procedure, radicals 1i and 1j were divided into three fragments: ${\bf A}{\bf u}^{+}$ ions with the ${\tt d}^{10}$ valence shell, an NN anion, and the closed-shell $\mathrm{PR}_{3}$ ligand; each respective dimer was divided into six fragments. To estimate the orbital contribution to $\mathrm{Au-P}.$ , Au− $C,$ and ${\bf A u-A u}$ bond energies, the second-order perturbation energy was calculated:  

$$
\Delta E_{\mathrm{DA}}^{(2)}=n_{\mathrm{D}}\langle\Phi_{\mathrm{D}}|\hat{F}|\Phi_{\mathrm{A}}\rangle^{2}/(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{D}}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{A}})
$$  

where $\Phi_{\mathrm{D}}$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{A}}$ as well as and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{D}}$ and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{A}}$ are the Lewis-type NBOs of donor (D) and non-Lewis type NBOs of an acceptor (A) and their energies, respectively; $n_{\mathrm{D}}$ is the occupation number of the donor NBO, and $\hat{\boldsymbol F}$ is a Fock operator.  

Parameters $J$ of the exchange interaction $\left({\cal H}=\,-2J\hat{\vec{S}}_{1}\hat{\vec{S}}_{2}\right)$ between radical fragments (1i and 1j) of dimers and between radical fragments of neighboring dimers were calculated by the spin-unrestricted broken-symmetry approach51 at the UB3LYP/def2-TZVP (with ECP for Au) level using the formula52  

$$
\boldsymbol{J}=\frac{\boldsymbol{E}^{\mathrm{HS}}-\boldsymbol{E}_{\mathrm{BS}}^{\mathrm{LS}}}{\langle\boldsymbol{S}^{2}\rangle^{\mathrm{HS}}-\langle\boldsymbol{S}^{2}\rangle_{\mathrm{BS}}^{\mathrm{LS}}}
$$  

where indices HS and LS correspond to high-spin and brokensymmetry low-spin solutions, respectively. Singlet−triplet splitting in the diradicals $\left(E_{\mathrm{ST}}\,=\,2\ \right)$ ) was also calculated by the CASSCF and CASSCF/NEVPT2 procedures53,54 with the same basis set.  

Electronic absorption spectra of radicals 1i,j were calculated via the time-dependent density functional theory (DFT) approach55 with the B3LYP functional and the def2-TZVP basis set (with ECP for Au). Most of the aforementioned calculations were performed using the ORCA 4.2.1 software suite.56  

The hyperfine coupling (HFC) tensor and constant $(A_{\mathrm{iso}})$ with a gold nucleus as well as with nitrogen and phosphorus nuclei were calculated by the exact two-component relativistic X2C method57,58 with a self-consistent account of spin−orbit coupling (SOC) implemented in the ADF2019 software package.59−61 These computations were performed with the B3LYP functional, the STOtype TZ2P-J basis set,62 and the nonpoint model for nuclei.63 A collinear approximation of spin polarization was used in the case of the SOC.64 The spherical Gaussian nuclear charge distribution model65 was utilized for accurate predictions of the HFC constants.  

# RESULTS AND DISCUSSION  

To date, a fairly wide range of mononuclear $\mathrm{Au}(\mathrm{I})$ derivatives of a nitronyl nitroxide radical $\mathbf{1a}{\mathrm{-}}\mathbf{h},2,$ , and 3 has been obtained (Scheme 2). An inspection of their crystallographic data did not reveal the presence of $\mathrm{Au(I)-Au(I)}$ bonds (Table 1). Indeed, in all cases, the distance between the gold centers is noticeably larger than the sum of their van der Waals (vdW) radii proposed by Alvarez (4.64 Å).66  

On the other hand, in the crystalline state, the vast majority of linear gold(I) complexes were found to be in the form of oligomers or polymers. The most common oligomers turned out to be dimers, trimers, and tetramers, whereas onedimensional chains are the most common polymers. The complexes remain in the monomeric form only when steric bulkiness of the substituents at the gold center prevents the Au−Au bonding.4 Therefore, to increase the propensity of $\mathrm{NN-Au-PR}_{3}$ complexes for the Au−Au bonding, we decided to synthesize the $\mathrm{NN-Au-P}^{n}\mathrm{Bu}_{3}$ (1i) complex, in which instead of bulky tert-butyl groups, the phosphorus atom is surrounded by $n$ -butyl substituents. The choice of the structure of the second compound is not so obvious and is rather the fruit of intuition. For instance, it has been noticed that in a series of $\mathrm{Ar-Au-PPh}_{3}$ complexes (4), the introduction of substituent(s) R having a positive M effect, e.g., a methoxy group or a fluorine atom, leads to the formation of dimers $\bar{\{\mathrm{Ar-Au}\mathrm{-}\mathrm{PPh}_{3}\}}_{2}$ through the $\mathrm{{Au}(I)\cdots{A u}(I)}$ bonding in solid phases.67 We thought that it is expedient to proceed in a similar way, namely, to synthesize the derivative $\mathrm{NN-Au-}$ $\mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{F}}\mathrm{Ph}_{3}$ (1j) containing para-fluorinated phenyl rings and to study its crystal structure.

Gold derivatives of the nitronyl nitroxide, $\mathrm{NN-Au-P}^{n}\mathrm{Bu}_{3}$ (1i) and $\mathrm{NN-Au-P}^{\mathrm{F}}\mathrm{Ph}_{3}$ (1j), were obtained in a high yield $(95{-}98\%)$ upon treatment with a base solution $(\bar{\mathrm{NaOH}}/\$ MeOH) containing equivalent amounts of $\mathrm{NN{-}H}$ and $C1-$ $\mathrm{Au-PR}_{3}$ generated in situ from chloro(tetrahydrothiophene) gold and the corresponding phosphine (Scheme 3). Analytically pure samples of complexes 1i,j were prepared by chromatography on silica gel and recrystallization from solvents suitable for the growth of crystals.  

To understand in detail the electronic structure of the gold derivatives of nitronyl nitroxide radicals, a number of experimental and theoretical methods were employed. Figure 1 shows experimental EPR spectra of radicals 1i and 1j, as recorded in dilute degassed toluene solutions at room temperature, and their simulation. Isotropic $\mathfrak{g}$ values were determined using solid DPPH as a reference, and accuracy levels of determining HFC interaction constants and $\mathfrak{g}$ values were estimated as $0.005\,\mathrm{~mT~}$ and 0.0001, respectively. The spectra of 1i and 1j are very similar and are characterized by predominant couplings with two equivalent nitrogen atoms and one phosphorus $\bar{(I=1/2)}$ , producing a quintet of doublets with slightly higher ${\tt g}$ values than typical for purely organic $\mathrm{R}-$ NNs.25 As in earlier studies,14 the spectra showed no signs of couplings with gold, although the only stable natural isotope 197Au is magnetic with I = 3/2.

To understand the absence of a hyperfine interaction with $^{197}\mathrm{Au},$ or at least of its manifestation in the EPR spectrum, we calculated spin-Hamiltonian parameters by the exact twocomponent relativistic X2C method with a self-consistent account of the SOC at the DFT level. Because the EPR spectra of radicals 1i and 1j are very similar (Figure 1), we performed the computations only for the optimized structure of 1i. These calculations predicted the following parameters for 1i: $g_{\mathrm{iso}}~=$ 2.0099, $A_{\mathrm{iso}}\mathrm{\bar{(}2\;\;N)}\;=\;0.584\;\;\mathrm{mT},$ $\mathit{\bar{A}_{\mathrm{iso}}(P)}\ =\ 0.410\ \mathrm{\mT},$ , and $A_{\mathrm{iso}}\big(^{197}\mathrm{Au}\big)\,=\,0.535\,\ \mathrm{mT}.$ . A comparison with the experiment showed that $g_{\mathrm{iso}}$ is noticeably overestimated, whereas $\bar{A}_{\mathrm{iso}}(\bf N)$ is reproduced fairly well, althoughas usual at the DFT level25it is slightly underestimated, and $A_{\mathrm{iso}}(\mathrm{P})$ is almost twice the experimental value. Most challenging, $A_{\mathrm{iso}}(^{197}\mathrm{Au})$ was predicted to be quite high.  

It should be noted that our computational approach recently manifested perfect agreement with the experiment for the HFC tensor with $^{125}\mathrm{{Te},\ ^{\circ}\mathrm{{^{187}R e,}}}$ and ${}^{127}\mathrm{I}$ nuclei.22,68,69 It is also important to mention that hyperfine interactions with the above-mentioned relativistic nuclei have been recorded either only at cryogenic temperatures68,69 or at elevated temperatures.22 The last result was explained by assuming substantially different characteristic times for averaging the anisotropy of the hyperfine-interaction tensors with nonrelativistic and relativistic nuclei: for the former, the anisotropy is due to the dipole−dipole interaction, whereas for the latter, a significant contribution to the anisotropy is due to SOC.22 It cannot be ruled out that in our case too, the hyperfine interaction with $^{197}\mathrm{Au}$ does not manifest itself as a splitting in the EPR spectrum owing to the slow averaging of anisotropic interactions arising from SOC.  

Figure 2 displays UV−vis spectra of radicals 1i,j. Both spectra have similar long-wavelength bands with pronounced fine structure and maxima at ${\sim}570\ \mathrm{\nm}$ . Both spectra also contain a highly intense band with maxima at 363 and $372~\mathrm{nm}$ for 1i and 1j, respectively. It is clear that at shorter wavelengths $\left(<\!250\ensuremath{\,\mathrm{~nm}}\right)$ , radical 1j with the $\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{Ar})_{3}$ substituent absorbs much more strongly due to the participation in electronic transitions of the aryl group. It is well known25 that UV−vis spectra of 2-substituted nitronyl nitroxides with alkyl, aryl, or heterocyclic substituents $\scriptstyle\left({\mathrm{NN}}-{\mathrm{R}}\right)$ yield a long-wavelength absorption band with clear-cut fine structure and a peak at $560{-}650\ \mathrm{nm}$ . To explain this similarity, we performed the time-dependent DFT calculation of the UV−vis spectrum of radical 1i.  

Figure 2 shows that the calculations well reproduce the UV− vis spectrum of 1i. As in the case of ${\mathrm{NN-R}}$ (Alk, Ar, or heterocycles),25,70 only one electronic transition is responsible for the long-wavelength band with the maximum at $570\ \mathrm{nm}$ . This transition involves a sum of the promotions of an $\alpha$ - electron from the singly occupied molecular orbital (SOMO) to the lowest unoccupied orbital (a) and of a $\beta$ -electron from the HOMO to the $\beta$ counterpart of the SOMO (b) (Figure 3). Thus, the fine structure of this band results from vibrational progression. Similarly, to a pyrazolyl-substituted NN, for which the $C{-}C$ stretching mode appears in the spectrum,70 progression of the $\scriptstyle{C-\mathrm{Au}}$ stretching vibration is most likely observed for 1i. The main contribution to the second band with a maximum at $363\,\mathrm{\nm}$ is made by a similar electronic transition: a negative combination of (a) and (b). Note that two $n{-}\pi^{*}$ transitions were predicted at 430 and $377\ \mathrm{nm}$ but with very low oscillator strength $(0.9~\mathrm{~to~}~2.9)~\times~10^{-4},$ ; accordingly, there is no sign of them in the spectrum (Figure 2). Thus, the electronic structure of the gold-substituted NN is very similar to that of ${\mathrm{NN-R}}$ $\mathrm{\bfR=Alk},$ Ar, or a heterocycle).

For open-shell systems, such as radicals 1i,j, the energies of SOMOs and LUMOs predetermine the redox properties of the compounds. We, therefore, investigated oxidation potentials for the synthesized nitroxides by cyclic voltammetry using a three-electrode system with $\mathrm{Fc/Fc^{+}}$ as the reference. Figure 4 shows a cyclic voltammogram of 1i covering the potential range available in acetonitrile as a solvent. One can see that the first one-electron oxidation peak at $0.11\;\mathrm{V}$ , assigned to the NN moiety, is accompanied by two response peaks. This means that the product of electrochemical oxidation of 1i (the corresponding cation $\mathbf{li}^{+}$ ) enters into a subsequent reversible chemical reaction with the formation of another electrochemically active compound $\mathbf{li}^{+\prime}$ , which, like $\mathbf{li}^{+}$ , is reduced during reverse potential scanning. At various potential sweep rates (0.05 to $1.0\,\,\mathrm{V/s},$ ), the interval between the direct and first response peaks proved to be $0.059\ \mathrm{V},$ , and the sum of currents in the response of reduction peaks to the current in the direct oxidation peak does not depend on the potential sweep rate and was found to be 0.96, which indicates electrochemical reversibility of the process (Figure S3). Aside from the said process, the cyclic-voltammetry curve also reflects irreversible electrochemical processes occurring at potentials of 2.29 and $-1.68\mathrm{~V~}$ (Figure 4).

Single crystals of complexes $\mathrm{NN-Au-P}^{n}\mathrm{Bu}_{3}$ (1i) and NN− $\mathrm{Au-P}^{\mathrm{Y}}\mathrm{Ph}_{3}$ (1j) (as crystallohydrate $\scriptstyle\mathbf{1}\mathbf{j}\cdot2\mathbf{H}_{2}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{O}}_{\mathrm{,}}^{\mathrm{\,v}}$ ) were obtained by recrystallization from a mixture of $\mathrm{CH}_{2}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ with $n$ -heptane or a mixture of acetone with $n$ -heptane, respectively. Inspection of the crystallographic data for the presence of the $\bar{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{u}(\mathrm{I})\cdots$ $\mathrm{Au}(\mathrm{I})$ bonding in the crystal structures of 1i and 1j uncovered the formation of dimerized molecules. Structures of dimerized radicals $\{\mathbf{1}\}_{2}$ and $\{{\bf1}{\bf j}\}_{2}$ are shown in Figure 5.  

In dimers $\{\mathbf{1}\}_{2}$ and $\{\mathbf{1}\}_{2}^{},$ the coordination polyhedron of Au atoms could be described as T-shaped. Indeed, $\mathrm{C}2{\mathrm{-}}{\mathrm{Au-P}}$ angles were found to be close to $180^{\circ}$ , and $\mathrm{P-Au-Au^{\prime}{-}P^{\prime}}$ dihedral angles close to $90^{\circ}$ (Table 3). C2−Au bond lengths for complexes 1i,j turned out to be $2.035{-}2.040\mathrm{~\AA},$ which are similar to those of previously described $\mathrm{NN-Au-PPh}_{3}$ radicals (1a) (2.03 Å).12 N−O bond lengths for the NN moieties proved to be $1.28\!-\!1.30\textrm{\textbar{A}},$ which are typical of the corresponding bond lengths of NN radicals.71  

Figure 6 shows a fragment of the crystal structure of the $\mathrm{NN-Au-P^{F}P h}_{3}\cdot2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}$ hydrate complex $\left({\bf1}\mathrm{j}{\cdot}2{\bf H}_{2}\mathrm{O}\right)$ . In 1j dimers, the angle between the planes of the paramagnetic moieties is $7^{\circ}$ , and the shortest $\mathrm{o}...\mathrm{o}$ distance is $3.376\,\mathrm{~\AA~}$ which is considerably greater than the sum of vdW radii of O atoms $(3.04\,\mathrm{~\normalfont~\AA~}^{72})$ . Dimers $\{\mathbf{1}\}\}_{2}$ and disordered water molecules (Figure 6) are assembled into a chain via $\mathrm{O_{NO}cdots}$ H−OH hydrogen bonds $\mathrm{\Delta(d(O{\cdots}H)}~=~1.89~\mathrm{~\AA},$ , $\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{O}_{\cdots}\mathrm{O})$ 2.713(3) Å, $\angle\mathrm{O}{\cdots}\mathrm{H}{-}\mathrm{O}\ =\ 167^{\circ}$ , on average) and short contacts $\mathrm{O_{H2O}...H^{\prime}{}_{C H3}}$ (2.16 Å) and $\mathrm{{H_{H2O}...O_{\Delta N O}^{\prime}}}$ (2.37 Å). In other words, these moieties lie opposite each other, formally giving rise to a columnar structure extended along the c direction. The formation of such a crystal structure was driven by the inclusion of water molecules, which was ensured by the growth of crystals in the acetone $-n$ - heptane system. By contrast, when the mixture of $\mathrm{CH}_{2}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ and $n$ -heptane was used, complex 1j precipitated as oily droplets.  

Well-shaped crystals of compound $\mathrm{NN-Au-P}^{n}\mathrm{Bu}_{3}$ (1i) were obtained in the mixture of $\mathrm{CH}_{2}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ and $n$ -heptane.

According to XRD data, in dimers $\left\{\mathbf{li}\right\}_{2},$ the angle between the planes of the nitronyl nitroxide moieties is $10^{\circ}$ , and the shortest $\mathrm{o}{\cdot}{\cdot}\mathrm{O}$ distance is $3.777\,\mathring{\mathrm{A}},$ , which is considerably larger than the sum of vdW radii of O atoms (3.04 Å). Given that there are no water molecules in the structure of complex 1i, its packing differs significantly from that of compound 1j (Figure 7).  

Nevertheless, the most interesting for us is spatial and electronic structure of dimers $\{\mathbf{1}\}_{2}$ and $\{{\bf1}{\bf j}\}_{2}$ . Distances between the gold centers in these dimers (3.095 and $2.930\mathrm{~\AA~}$ for $\{\mathbf{1}\}\}_{2}$ and $\{\mathbf{i}\}_{2},$ respectively) are less than the sum of their vdW radii but greater than the sum of their covalent radii (2.72 Å). It should be pointed out that according to different approaches, several values of the vdW radius for gold have been proposed so far, and they range from previously established $1.66\,\mathring{\mathrm{A}}^{73}$ to recently accepted $2.32\,\mathring{\mathrm{A}}.^{66}$ To confirm the presence of weak noncovalent bonding interactions in the dimers $\{\mathbf{1}\}_{2}$ and $\{{\bf1}{\bf j}\}_{2}$ and to estimate their energy, we carried out all-electron DFT calculations with the scalar relativistic DKH2 Hamiltonian as described in the Computational Details Section, using which we performed the QTAIM and NBO analyses. Because the C−Au and P−Au coordination bonds in paramagnetic species are still studied insufficiently, we also characterized these bonds. As expected, the $(3,-1)$ BCPs were localized for $\mathrm{Au-P}$ and ${\mathrm{Au-C}}$ coordination bonds, but more importantly, the QTAIM topological analysis did reveal the presence of the $(3,\ -1)$ BCPs for the $\mathrm{Au(I)-Au(I)}$ bonding in the 1i and 1j crystal structures (Table 4). The coordination bonds $\mathrm{Au}{-}\mathrm{P}$ and Au− C are characterized by moderate electron densities (0.11− 0.13), rather large and positive values of their Laplacian, and negative electronic energy densities at BCPs. These values of the above-mentioned topological descriptors are typical of donor−acceptor bonds between the seemingly closed-shell $\mathrm{Au}(\mathrm{I})$ center and NN or phosphine ligands. Note that $|V_{\mathrm{b}}|/G_{\mathrm{b}}$ values are in the range between 1 and 2, implying their partial covalence.74,75  

The Au−Au bonding in crystal structures of 1i and 1j features low electron densities, low positive values of their Laplacian, and close-to-zero or zero energy density at the BCPs (Table 4), which is typical of metal−metal bonding in similar chemical systems.76−78 The low values of the aforementioned topological descriptors for Au−Au bonds are in line with the low Mayer bond orders calculated for them (Table 4).  

With the help of the NBO analysis and second-order perturbation theory, we estimated the orbital contribution to the energies of the bonds in question. Note that these values are used only to qualitatively assess the relative strength of different bond types. Table 4 shows that the orbital contribution to the ${\mathrm{Au-C}}$ bond energy is huge, whereas the orbital contribution to the $\mathrm{Au-P}$ bond energy is noticeably smaller. In both cases, the predominant contribution is made by one pair of orbitals. Specifically, the interaction of the lone pair of the $\mathrm{P}$ atom (donor) with the 6s orbital on $\mathrm{Au}(\mathrm{I})$ (acceptor) makes ${\sim}95\%$ of the total orbital contribution to the $\mathrm{Au-P}$ bond energy. A similar proportion $(\sim\!95\%)$ is given by the interaction of the lone pair on the C atom (NN group) with the $\mathrm{Au}(\mathrm{I})$ 6s orbital.  

In the case of the Au−Au bonding, the orbital contribution is very small, which, along with other parameters listed in Table 4, points to low energy of this bond. In this context, the main contribution comes from the interaction of the 5d orbitals of one $\mathrm{Au}(\mathrm{I})$ ion with the 6s orbital of another ${\mathrm{Au}}(\mathrm{I})$ .  

The structures of $\{\mathbf{i}\}_{2}$ and $\{{\bf1}{\bf j}\}_{2}$ were optimized at the B97- D3 level both in the gas phase and in dichloromethane and acetonitrile solutions. In all cases, the structures of both dimers correspond to minima on potential-energy surfaces. For dimer $\{\mathbf{i}\}_{2},$ predicted Gibbs free energy of formation $(\Delta G_{\mathrm{f}}^{0})$ is $-5.1$ and $-4.6\;\mathrm{kcal/mol}$ in $\mathrm{CH}_{2}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{CN}_{:}$ , respectively. These values of $\Delta G_{\mathrm{f}}^{0}$ correspond to $K_{\mathrm{eq}}$ equal to $\sim\!5\stackrel{\cdot}{\times}10^{3}$ and ${\sim}2~\times$ $10^{3}~\mathrm{~M~}^{-1}$ at room temperature in $\mathrm{CH}_{2}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ and $\mathrm{CH}_{3}\mathrm{CN}$ respectively, and at a concentration of $\sim\!10^{-4}\mathrm{~\,~M~}$ , the monomeric form in solution is favored. As for $\{\mathbf{1}\}_{2}^{},$ more exothermic dimerization was predicted: computed $\Delta G_{\mathrm{f}}^{0}$ was $-8.9$ and $-7.7\ \mathrm{kcal/mol}$ , respectively. It must be mentioned that error in the calculation of $\Delta G_{\mathrm{f}}^{0}$ can be several $\mathrm{kcal/mol}.$ Therefore, the calculations do not contradict the crystallization of radicals 1i and 1j as dimers and their presence in dilute solutions in the monomeric form.  

As described above, in the $\mathrm{1j\cdot2H_{2}O}$ crystal structure, the dimers are assembled into chains, whereas in the 1i crystal structure, such chains are absent. To predict the magnetic properties of both paramagnets, intra- and interdimer exchange interactions were calculated at the BS-DFT level. For dimer $\{\mathbf{i}\}_{2},$ the calculations were also carried out at a higher level of theory. Table 5 shows that for this dimer, BS-DFT predicts a moderate antiferromagnetic exchange interaction $(J=-23.4$ ${\mathsf{c m}}^{-1},$ . This parameter may be somewhat underestimated here because the calculation at a higher level predicted a doubled value. For dimer $\{\mathbf{1}\}_{2}^{},$ BS-DFT predicted a much stronger antiferromagnetic interaction $(J=-95.8\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1})$ ), which may be attributed to both a more parallel arrangement of NN moieties and a shorter Au−Au bond. These factors influence the mechanisms of exchange interactions through space and through bonds, respectively.

# CONCLUSIONS  

The coordination chemistry of gold(I) has been a rapidly developing field in recent times, and the unique properties and applications of gold complexes, especially in catalysis, medicine, and materials chemistry, made a huge contribution to these scientific advances. A new research avenue has recently emerged that deals with the preparation of gold(I)  

derivatives carrying paramagnetic substituents as well as their application to the chemistry of stable nitroxides. In this regard, we designed and synthesized $\mathrm{Au}(\mathrm{I})$ mononuclear complexes $\left(\mathrm{NN-Au}{-}\mathrm{PR}_{3}\right)$ with a nitronyl nitroxide anionic ligand and various neutral phosphine ligands $\left[n{\mathrm{-}}\mathrm{Bu}_{3}\mathrm{P}\right.$ or $(4\mathrm{-FC}_{6}\mathrm{H}_{4})_{3}\mathrm{P}]$ and studied their spatial and electronic structure and physicochemical properties. X-ray structural analysis of the new complexes revealed that during crystallization, the complexes tend to dimerize with the formation of $\mathrm{{Au}(I)-}$ $\mathrm{Au(\bar{I})}$ bonds (2.930 and 3.095 Å). According to quantum chemical calculations, the Au−Au bonds have a low energy, to which the main contribution is made by the interaction of the 5d orbitals of one $\mathrm{Au}(\mathrm{I})$ ion with the 6s orbital of another $\mathrm{Au}(\mathrm{I})$ . In the solid state, the Au−Au bonding leads to close proximity of two radical moieties that, in turn, gives rise to antiferromagnetic exchange interactions between paramagnetic centers. In general, this opens new possibilities in design of different exchange-coupled systems.
