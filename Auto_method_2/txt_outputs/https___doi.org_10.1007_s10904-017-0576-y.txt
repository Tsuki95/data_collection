## Abstract

Two Au(I) xanthates, Au2(S2COEt)2 and Au2(S2CO _i_ Pr)2, were synthesized and
the crystal structure of the former was determined by single-crystal X-ray
crystallography. This structure is compared to the published structure of
Au2(S2CO _i_ Pr)2 and of the closely-related dithiocarbamate Au2(S2CNEt2)2.
Raman spectra at variable temperature for both Au(I) xanthates and at variable
pressure for Au2(S2COEt)2 are presented. Raman spectra of Au2(S2COEt)2 at
variable temperature or pressure show no evidence of any phase transitions, in
agreement with the unit cell parameters measured in the 100–260 K temperature
range. Gas-phase DFT calculations for both Au(I) xanthates and for one Au(I)
dithiocarbamate Au2(S2CNEt2)2 were performed in order to assign experimental
Raman peaks.

## 1 Introduction

Gold(I) compounds are among the most attractive building blocks for
coordination polymers based on d-block metal ions
[[1](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR1 "M.-R. Azani, O. Castillo,
M.L. Gallego, T. Parella, G. Aullón, O. Crespo, A. Laguna, S. Alvarez, R. Mas-
Ballesté, F. Zamora, Chem. Eur. J. 18, 9965–9976 \(2012\)")]. Aurophilic
interactions [2,3,[4](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR4 "A.L. Balch,
Gold Bull. 37, 45–50 \(2004\)")] play a key role for determining
characteristics, in particular for luminescence properties in view of
applications as luminescent sensors
[[5](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR5 "M.J. Katz, T. Ramnial, H.-Z.
Yu, D.B. Leznoff, J. Am. Chem. Soc. 130, 10662–10673 \(2008\)")]. A wide
variety of structurally diverse gold(I) dithiocarbamates and xanthates have
been reported. They share one-dimensional gold(I) chains as a common motif,
leading to many different coordination polymers. Detailed structural
differences, such as gold(I)–gold(I) distances and gold(I)–gold(I)–gold(I)
[[6](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR6 "R.J. Roberts, N. Belanger-
Desmarais, C. Reber, D.B. Leznoff, Chem. Commun. 50, 3148–3150 \(2014\)")]
angles vary with the nature of the ligands and strongly influence the
properties of these inorganic polymers.

We present detailed Raman spectra at variable temperature and pressure to
illustrate differences between gold(I) dithiocarbamate and xanthate systems,
including a new crystal structure for one of the xanthates. DFT calculations
are used to assign Raman spectra and to compare the three structurally related
materials.

## 2 Experimental Section

### 2.1 Syntheses

All reagents were purchased from commercial sources and used as received
without further purification. Potassium ethylxanthogenate, K(S2COEt), and
(DMS)AuCl were purchased from Aldrich. Sodium isopropylxanthate, Na(S2CO _i_
Pr), was purchased from Pfaltz and Bauer. Acetone, dichloromethane and
dimethylsulfoxide were of certified ACS grade and were purchased from Fisher
Scientific.

#### 2.1.1 Synthesis of Au2(S2COEt)2

The synthetic procedure was adapted from the literature
[[7](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR7 "A.A. Mohamed, I. Kani, A.O.
Ramirez, J.P. Fackler, Inorg. Chem. 43, 3833–3839 \(2004\)")]. A first
solution of the ligand was prepared by dissolving K(S2COEt) (0.0994 g, 0.62
mmol) in acetone (21 mL) at room temperature while stirring. The solution was
then cooled to 0 °C in an ice/water bath. A second solution of the gold(I)
precursor was prepared by dissolving (DMS)AuCl (0.1895 g, 0.64 mmol) in DCM (9
mL) at room temperature while stirring. The second solution was then added
slowly and dropwise to the first solution at 0 °C while stirring. The
resulting solution rapidly turned light yellow and a precipitate formed. The
solution was left to stir for 95 min and was filtered on a Büchner funnel. The
precipitate was washed twice with distilled water and dried in a desiccator.
The product was collected as light yellow flakes/powder (0.1072 g, 0.17 mmol)
with a yield of 54%. Unsuccessful attempts to obtain X-ray quality crystals of
this compound are documented in the literature
[[7](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR7 "A.A. Mohamed, I. Kani, A.O.
Ramirez, J.P. Fackler, Inorg. Chem. 43, 3833–3839 \(2004\)")]. We were able to
obtain crystals suitable for an X-ray analysis through slow cooling of a
boiling DCM solution of the compound and subsequent slow evaporation at room
temperature. Raman (293 K, solid-state, cm−1): 205 (m), 305 (w), 393 (w), 431
(m), 467 (s), 544 (m), 645 (vs), 816 (vw), 858 (w), 1005 (w), 1022 (w), 1091
(w, br), 1113 (w), 1145 (vw), 1188 (w), 1209 (w), 1269 (vw), 1342 (vw), 1365
(w), 1390 (w), 1442 (w), 1455 (w), 1463 (w), 2717 (vw), 2882 (vw), 2917 (vw),
2945 (vw), 2955 (vw, sh), 2992 (vw). Single crystal X-ray diffraction for
C6H10Au2O2S4 (_M_ = 636.31): monoclinic, space group P21 (no. 4), _a_ =
6.9777(3) Å, _b_ = 7.4140(4) Å, _c_ = 11.8437(5) Å, _β_ = 100.440(2)°, _V_ =
602.56(5) Å3, _Z_ = 2, _T_ = 150 K, μ(CuKα) = 51.392 mm−1, _D_ _calc_ = 3.507
g/mm3, 19,082 reflections measured (7.59 ≤ 2Θ ≤ 140.796), 1841 unique (_R_ int
= 0.0546) which were used in all calculations. The final _R_ 1 was 0.0325 (I >
2σ(I)) and _wR_ 2 was 0.0844 (all data). CCDC 1533982 contains the
crystallographic data for this compound. The data can be obtained free of
charge via <http://www.ccdc.cam.ac.uk/conts/retrieving.html>, or from the
Cambridge Crystallographic Data Centre, 12 Union Road, Cambridge CB2 1EZ, UK;
fax: (+44) 1223 336 033; or e-mail: deposit@ccdc.cam.ac.uk.

#### 2.1.2 Synthesis of Au2(S2COiPr)2

Na(S2CO _i_ Pr) (0.0812 g, 0.51 mmol) was dissoved in DMSO while stirring at
room temperature. A solution of the gold(I) precursor was prepared by
dissolving at room temperature (DMS)AuCl (0.1484 g, 0.50 mmol) in a minimum of
acetone (about 15 mL) while stirring. This solution was then added dropwise to
the ligand solution at room temperature while stirring. Upon addition, the
resulting solution turned progressively orange and a precipitate formed. The
resulting solution was stirred for 5 min before it was filtered on a Büchner
funnel and the collected precipitate was washed four times with distilled
water to remove most DMSO. The precipitate was dried in a desiccator under
vacuum, affording orange lustrous crystalline flakes (0.0186 g, 0.03 mmol)
with a yield of 11%. Raman (293 K, solid-state, cm−1): 189 (m), 309 (vw), 360
(w), 390 (w), 419 (w), 469 (s), 489 (m), 548 (w), 642 (vs), 812 (w), 901 (w),
938 (vw), 1010 (w), 1097 (vw, br), 1138 (vw), 1191 (vw), 1220 (vw), 1324 (vw),
1338 (w), 1447 (vw, at 80 K), 1452 (vw). The crystal structure of Au2(S2CO _i_
Pr)2 is reported in the literature
[[1](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR1 "M.-R. Azani, O. Castillo,
M.L. Gallego, T. Parella, G. Aullón, O. Crespo, A. Laguna, S. Alvarez, R. Mas-
Ballesté, F. Zamora, Chem. Eur. J. 18, 9965–9976 \(2012\)")].

#### 2.1.3 Single Crystal X-ray Diffraction Determination

Diffraction measurements on a single crystal of Au2(S2COEt)2 were made on a
Bruker Microstar X8 diffractometer. The crystal was kept at 150 K during data
collection. Using Olex2 [[8](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR8 "O.V.
Dolomanov, L.J. Bourhis, R.J. Gildea, J.A.K. Howard, H. Puschmann, J. Appl.
Cryst. 42, 339–341 \(2009\)")], the structure was solved with the XM
[[9](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR9 "G. Sheldrick, Acta
Crystallogr. A 64, 112–122 \(2008\)")] structure solution program using Dual
Space and refined with the XL [[9](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR9
"G. Sheldrick, Acta Crystallogr. A 64, 112–122 \(2008\)")] refinement package
using least squares minimisation. In addition, unit cell parameters were
measured at 100, 140, 180, 220 and 260 K (Online Resource, Table S7).

#### 2.2.1 Au2(S2COEt)2

A ground-state geometry optimization was carried out in the gas phase for a
molecular unit of formula Au2(S2COEt)2 using the crystal structure reported
here as a starting point. The geometry optimization was conducted with the
hybrid DFT exchange–correlation functional PBE1PBE
[[12](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR12 "C. Adamo, V. Barone, J.
Chem. Phys. 110, 6158–6170 \(1999\)")] along with the relativistic basis set
Lanl2dz [[13](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR13 "P.J. Hay, W.R.
Wadt, J. Chem. Phys. 82, 270–283 \(1985\)")] with effective core potentials
for all atoms with the default convergence criterion. The obtained optimized
structure was used for a subsequent frequency calculation at the same level of
theory, which revealed no imaginary frequencies. Interestingly, the optimized
structure has a S–S–S–S dihedral angle of 36°, which differs from the dihedral
angle in the crystal structure (3°). The calculated intramolecular Au–Au
distance is 2.869 Å, close to the crystal structure value of 2.8292(7) Å.
Molecular orbitals were calculated for this and all following optimized
structures.

#### 2.2.2 Au2(S2CO _i_ Pr)2

Again, a ground-state geometry optimization was carried out in the gas phase
for a molecular unit of formula Au2(S2CO _i_ Pr)2 using the crystal structure
[[1](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR1 "M.-R. Azani, O. Castillo,
M.L. Gallego, T. Parella, G. Aullón, O. Crespo, A. Laguna, S. Alvarez, R. Mas-
Ballesté, F. Zamora, Chem. Eur. J. 18, 9965–9976 \(2012\)")] as a starting
point. The geometry optimization was conducted with the same method as
Au2(S2COEt)2. The obtained optimized structure was used for a subsequent
frequency calculation, which revealed one imaginary frequency. The calculated
S–S–S–S dihedral angle is approximately 0°, identical to the crystal
structure, and the calculated Au–Au intramolecular distance is 2.884 Å. The
imaginary frequency mode brings the S atoms out of plane, leading to S–S–S–S
dihedral angles different from zero.

This optimized structure was used for a second ground-state geometry
optimization at the same level of theory with a “VeryTight” convergence
criterion and a “UltraFine” integration grid. A frequency calculation was
performed with the newly-optimized structure and showed no imaginary
frequencies. It is noteworthy that this optimized structure has now a S–S–S–S
dihedral angle of 39° and a shorter calculated Au–Au intramolecular distance
of 2.863 Å.

#### 2.2.3 {Au2(S2CO _i_ Pr)2}2

A ground-state geometry optimization was carried out in the gas-phase for two
molecular units of formula {Au2(S2CO _i_ Pr)2}2, again using the crystal
structure [[1](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR1 "M.-R. Azani, O.
Castillo, M.L. Gallego, T. Parella, G. Aullón, O. Crespo, A. Laguna, S.
Alvarez, R. Mas-Ballesté, F. Zamora, Chem. Eur. J. 18, 9965–9976 \(2012\)")]
as a starting point. The geometry optimization was conducted with the
methodology described for Au2(S2COEt)2 and the frequency calculation revealed
no imaginary frequencies. The calculated S–S–S–S dihedral angle is
approximately 40° (0° in the crystal structure) and the calculated Au–Au intra
and intermolecular distances are 2.871 and 3.052 Å, respectively, (2.8066(7)
and 3.0176(7) Å in the crystal structure).

#### 2.2.4 Au2(S2CNEt2)2

In order to compare with a related dithiocarbamate system, a ground-state
geometry optimization was carried out in the gas-phase for a molecular unit of
formula Au2(S2CNEt2)2 using the crystal structure
[[14](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR14 "D.D. Heinrich, J.-C. Wang,
J.P. Fackler, Jr, Acta Crystallogr. C 46, 1444–1447 \(1990\)")] as a starting
point. Again, the theoretical method used revealed no imaginary frequencies.
Interestingly, the optimized structure has a S–S–S–S dihedral angle of 48°,
which is close to the dihedral angle in the crystal structure (40°). The
calculated Au–Au intramolecular distance is 2.843 Å, comparable to the value
of 2.782(1) Å from the crystal structure
[[14](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR14 "D.D. Heinrich, J.-C. Wang,
J.P. Fackler, Jr, Acta Crystallogr. C 46, 1444–1447 \(1990\)")].

## 3 Results and Discussion

### 3.1 Crystal Structure of Au2(S2COEt)2

Au2(S2COEt)2 crystallizes in the monoclinic space group P21 with 2 molecules
in the unit cell. The structure contains molecular units of formula
Au2(S2COEt)2, shown in Fig. [1](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#Fig1),
where 2 xanthate ligands are bridging the two Au(I) centers, leading to the
formation of an intramolecular aurophilic interaction with a distance of
2.8292(7) Å and of a typical Au2S4C2 eight-membered ring
[[1](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR1 "M.-R. Azani, O. Castillo,
M.L. Gallego, T. Parella, G. Aullón, O. Crespo, A. Laguna, S. Alvarez, R. Mas-
Ballesté, F. Zamora, Chem. Eur. J. 18, 9965–9976 \(2012\)"),
[7](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR7 "A.A. Mohamed, I. Kani, A.O.
Ramirez, J.P. Fackler, Inorg. Chem. 43, 3833–3839 \(2004\)")]. All atoms of
this eight-membered ring are coplanar. The average Au–S bond length and S–Au–S
angle are 2.29 Å and 173° respectively. The shortest intermolecular Au–Au
distance between two molecular units is 3.6620(9) Å. Figure
[1](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#Fig1) shows the 1D zig-zag chain with
intermolecular Au–Au interactions. The angles between consecutive Au(I)
centers along this chain are approximately 163° and 72°, illustrating this
structural feature. It is noteworthy that the shortest intermolecular Au–Au
distance and the geometry of the 1D chain are similar to the propyl-
substituted analogue [[1](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR1 "M.-R.
Azani, O. Castillo, M.L. Gallego, T. Parella, G. Aullón, O. Crespo, A. Laguna,
S. Alvarez, R. Mas-Ballesté, F. Zamora, Chem. Eur. J. 18, 9965–9976
\(2012\)")] (3.636(1) Å, zig-zag chain with Au-Au-Au angles of ca. 168° and
71°) and to the butyl-substituted analogue
[[7](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR7 "A.A. Mohamed, I. Kani, A.O.
Ramirez, J.P. Fackler, Inorg. Chem. 43, 3833–3839 \(2004\)")] (3.6436(17) Å,
zig-zag chain with Au–Au–Au angles of ca. 167° and 70°). These features are
distinctly different from Au2(S2CO _i_ Pr)2, an Au(I) xanthate with kinked 1D
chains (Au–Au–Au angles of 148° along the chain) in the solid-state with a
much shorter intermolecular Au–Au distance (3.0176(7) Å) or to the quasi-
linear 1D chains in many gold(I) dithiocarbamates such as Au2(S2CNEt2)2 with
an intermolecular Au–Au separation of 3.004(1) Å.

The intramolecular Au–Au distance of 2.8292(7) Å is shorter than for the
parent compounds Au2(S2COPr)2 (2.8414(10) Å)
[[1](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR1 "M.-R. Azani, O. Castillo,
M.L. Gallego, T. Parella, G. Aullón, O. Crespo, A. Laguna, S. Alvarez, R. Mas-
Ballesté, F. Zamora, Chem. Eur. J. 18, 9965–9976 \(2012\)")] and Au2(S2COBu)2
(2.8494(15) Å) [[7](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR7 "A.A. Mohamed,
I. Kani, A.O. Ramirez, J.P. Fackler, Inorg. Chem. 43, 3833–3839 \(2004\)")],
but longer than for Au2(S2CO _i_ Pr)2 (2.8066(7) Å)
[[1](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR1 "M.-R. Azani, O. Castillo,
M.L. Gallego, T. Parella, G. Aullón, O. Crespo, A. Laguna, S. Alvarez, R. Mas-
Ballesté, F. Zamora, Chem. Eur. J. 18, 9965–9976 \(2012\)")] or Au2(S2CNEt2)2
(2.782(1) Å) [[14](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR14 "D.D. Heinrich,
J.-C. Wang, J.P. Fackler, Jr, Acta Crystallogr. C 46, 1444–1447 \(1990\)")].
However, Au–S, C–S or C–O bond lengths can not be distinguished within three
standard deviations from the systems mentioned above. Unfortunately, it is
also not possible to distinguish the C–O bond lengths in our system from the
C–N bond lengths in Au2(S2CNEt2)2.

The unit cell parameters were measured at different temperatures from 260 to
100 K. All unit cell parameters (a, b, c, β and V) vary in a linear fashion
over this temperature range and decrease upon cooling down the single crystal,
as shown in the Online Resource (Table S7; Fig. S1–S5). This continuous and
smooth decrease of the unit cell parameters suggests that no phase transitions
occur in this temperature range.

### 3.2 Raman Spectroscopy

Variable-temperature Raman spectra are shown in Figs.
[2](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#Fig2) and
[3](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#Fig3) for Au2(S2COEt)2 and Au2(S2CO _i_
Pr)2, respectively. Assignments are given in Online Resource 1, Tables S12 and
S13. Shifts of Raman peaks with temperature span the range +0.07 to −0.03
cm−1/K for Au2(S2COEt)2 and +0.009 to −0.03 cm1/K for Au2(S2CO _i_ Pr)2. The
negative slopes are typical for temperature-dependent Raman experiments
[[15](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR15 "Y. Suffren, F.-G. Rollet,
C. Reber, Comments. Inorg. Chem. 32, 246–276 \(2011\)")]. The positive slopes,
observed for the peak at approximately 550 cm−1 denoted by squares in Figs.
[2](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#Fig2) and
[3](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#Fig3) are unusual. Raman spectra for
both systems show linear, continuous shifts of the Raman peaks with
temperature. No appearance or disappearance of Raman peaks was observed,
suggesting that no phase transitions occur in the temperature range studied.

### 3.3 DFT Calculations

#### 3.3.1 Structure Optimization and Frequency Calculations

The optimized structures are in good agreement with experimental data (Online
resource 1, Tables S8–S11), except for the S–S–S–S dihedral angle for both
Au(I) xanthate systems. The calculated structures for Au2(S2COEt)2 and
Au2(S2CO _i_ Pr)2 have S atoms below and above the plane, even though the
experimental crystal structures show the four sulphur atoms in the plane of
the Au2S4C2 eight-membered ring. It was possible for the _i_ -Pr-substituted
system to obtain a DFT-optimized structure in the gas-phase with four S atoms
in the same plane (saddle point, 1 imaginary frequency) and another structure
with out-of-plane S atoms (minimum, no imaginary frequency). Interestingly,
the saddle-point structure (four sulphur atoms in a same plane) possesses an
intramolecular Au–Au distance of 2.884 Å, longer than for the minimum-energy
structure (sulphur atoms below and above the plane, 2.863 Å). The calculated
Gibbs free energy for the conversion of the saddle-point structure to the
calculated minimum is approximately −8 kJ/mol (gas-phase, PBE1PBE/Lanl2dz, T =
298.15 K). This distortion of the ligand can be a means of achieving a
shorter, more favorable Au–Au distance, while also relieving S–S repulsion
[[1](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR1 "M.-R. Azani, O. Castillo,
M.L. Gallego, T. Parella, G. Aullón, O. Crespo, A. Laguna, S. Alvarez, R. Mas-
Ballesté, F. Zamora, Chem. Eur. J. 18, 9965–9976 \(2012\)")] which increases
as the Au–Au distance decreases for four planar S atoms. This capability of
the ligand to distort to achieve a shorter M–M distance has already been
reported for Ir2(dimen4)2+ complexes
[[16](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR16 "B.M. Hunter, R.M.
Villahermosa, C.L. Exstrom, M.G. Hill, K.R. Mann, H.B. Gray, Inorg. Chem. 51,
6898–6905 \(2012\)")]. This observation of a distortion of the ligand with
out-of-plane sulphur atoms is observed in several crystal structures of Au(I)
xanthates or analogues [[1](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR1 "M.-R.
Azani, O. Castillo, M.L. Gallego, T. Parella, G. Aullón, O. Crespo, A. Laguna,
S. Alvarez, R. Mas-Ballesté, F. Zamora, Chem. Eur. J. 18, 9965–9976
\(2012\)"), [14](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR14 "D.D. Heinrich,
J.-C. Wang, J.P. Fackler, Jr, Acta Crystallogr. C 46, 1444–1447 \(1990\)")].
However, the threshold Au–Au distance at which this deformation occurs is
difficult to identify, as it is dependent on several intra and possibly
intermolecular interactions.

A comparison of experimental Raman spectra with DFT-calculated spectra is
given in Fig. [6](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#Fig6) for individual
complexes of both xanthates and for a dithiocarbamate, Au2(S2CNEt2)2, with
ligand substituents identical to the xanthate illustrated in Fig.
[1](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#Fig1). The overall agreement between
calculated and experimental spectra is satisfactory, but definitely better for
the dithiocarbamate than for the xanthates.

#### 3.3.2 Frontier Molecular Orbitals

Frontier molecular orbitals were calculated for both xanthate systems in the
gas-phase at the PBE1PBE/Lanl2dz level of theory and shown in Fig.
[1](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#Fig1). Considering a single molecular
unit, DFT calculations reveal similar HOMO and LUMO characteristics for both
Au(I) xanthates (Fig. [7](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#Fig7)). The HOMO
is mainly centered on the Au(I) centers with some contributions of the S atoms
and possesses a Au(I)-Au(I) σ* character. In contrast, the LUMO is centered on
the S2C-O fragment of the xanthate moiety and is π* in nature. Therefore, for
a single molecular unit, the lowest energy excited state is calculated to be a
metal-metal-to-ligand charge transfer (MMLCT) state. The calculated HOMO–LUMO
gap is similar for both systems in this situation (ca. 36,700 cm−1, Fig.
[8](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#Fig8)), but represents better the
Au2(S2COEt)2 system, with long intermolecular Au–Au contacts in the solid-
state (3.66 Å). Consequently, we expect for Au2(S2COEt)2 a high-lying MMLCT
excited state, a conclusion supported by the absence of luminescence in the
visible wavelength range at room temperature upon excitation with a 488 nm
laser. This is in stark contrast to the corresponding Au(I) dithiocarbamate
Au2(S2CNEt2)2 with short Au–Au intermolecular contacts (ca. 3.00 Å)
[[14](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR14 "D.D. Heinrich, J.-C. Wang,
J.P. Fackler, Jr, Acta Crystallogr. C 46, 1444–1447 \(1990\)")] and a
luminescence centered at approximately 550 nm
[[17](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR17 "F. Baril-Robert, M.A.
Radtke, C. Reber, J. Phys. Chem. C 116, 2192–2197 \(2012\)")] in the same
conditions. Given that Au2(S2CO _i_ Pr)2 possesses also short Au–Au
intermolecular contacts (ca. 3.02 Å)
[[1](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR1 "M.-R. Azani, O. Castillo,
M.L. Gallego, T. Parella, G. Aullón, O. Crespo, A. Laguna, S. Alvarez, R. Mas-
Ballesté, F. Zamora, Chem. Eur. J. 18, 9965–9976 \(2012\)")], the
intermolecular aurophilic interaction needs to be taken into account
explicitly in the DFT calculations to correctly describe the nature of the
frontier molecular orbitals.

DFT calculations on a pair of molecular units were attempted for both Au(I)
xanthates. For Au2(S2COEt)2, the intermolecular Au–Au distance in the crystal
structure is about 3.66 Å, corresponding to a weak aurophilic interaction. A
geometry optimization with the same method failed to yield an optimized
structure in good agreement with the experiment and tended to significantly
shorten the intermolecular Au–Au distance. For Au2(S2CO _i_ Pr)2, the
intermolecular aurophilic interaction is stronger and the geometry
optimization successfully converged to an acceptable structure. The HOMO for a
pair of molecular units of Au2(S2CO _i_ Pr)2 is located predominantly on the
Au(I) centers with some contributions from the S atoms and displays a
Au(I)–Au(I) σ* character for the intra- and intermolecular aurophilic
interactions, as illustrated in Fig.
[7](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#Fig7). The LUMO is mainly centered on
the Au(I) centers involved in the intermolecular aurophilic interaction with
some electronic density on the S2C–O fragments of the xanthate moiety. Unlike
the HOMO, the LUMO rather possesses a Au(I)–Au(I) σ character for the
intermolecular aurophilic interaction (Fig.
[7](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#Fig7)). When a pair of molecular units
with a significant intermolecular aurophilic interaction is considered, the
lowest-energy excited state corresponds to a metal–metal centered (MMC) state.
The nature of the HOMO and the LUMO for this pair of molecular units resembles
the reported calculations for a pair of molecular units of Au2(S2CNEt2)2
[[17](/article/10.1007/s10904-017-0576-y#ref-CR17 "F. Baril-Robert, M.A.
Radtke, C. Reber, J. Phys. Chem. C 116, 2192–2197 \(2012\)")]. DFT
calculations illustrate the defining influence of the metal–metal chain
geometry for the frontier orbital characteristics.

Raman spectroscopy reveals subtle but quantitative differences between gold(I)
xanthates and related dithiocarbamates, leading to unusual shifts of some
frequencies at variable temperature and pressure, key characteristics beyond
the crystal structure.
